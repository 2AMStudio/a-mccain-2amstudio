Architectural Approach & Research Focus

My work explores the intersection of spatial design, social dynamics, and functional adaptability in conference environments. Drawing from frameworks including Lefebvre's spatial triad and Foucault's heterotopias, I examine how designed spaces mediate authority, facilitate collaboration, and adapt to changing conditions.

Double-Design for Durable Architecture

Developing architectural solutions that balance immediate functionality with long-term adaptability, creating spaces that remain relevant through changing social and technological contexts.

Long-life Fabric & Function

Focusing on creating spatial frameworks that democratize access and use, ensuring environments serve diverse communities and adapt to evolving needs without requiring complete reconstruction.

Event Coordination in Architectural Ethos

Integrating event design principles into architectural practice, recognizing that spaces must facilitate human interaction, knowledge exchange, and collaborative experiences.

Adaptive Hybrid Spaces

Designing environments that seamlessly integrate physical and digital experiences, creating inclusive platforms for global collaboration that transcend traditional spatial limitations.

Research Insights: Post-Pandemic Conference Spaces

My research examines how conference centers mediated authority and facilitated collaboration during the COVID-19 pandemic. The study reveals the duality of these spaces as sites of both institutional control and unplanned creativity, highlighting how spatial arrangements directly influence idea flow and hierarchy navigation.

This work argues for reimagining knowledge exchange environments through inclusive, adaptable design approaches that incorporate emerging technologies like VR and biophilic design while promoting genuine global connectivity.

Research & Publications

Double-Design for Durable Architecture: Long-life Fabric and Function for the Democratisation of Space
2024

This dissertation examines the socio-spatial reality of conference centres through the lens of the COVID-19 pandemic, using Lefebvre's spatial triad, Massey's 'throwntogetherness', and Foucault's theory of heterotopias as central frameworks. The research traces how these spaces have mediated authority and facilitated collaboration to adapt to rapidly changing conditions, revealing the duality of conference centres as places of institutional control and sites of unplanned creativity.
